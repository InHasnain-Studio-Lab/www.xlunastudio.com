Make laptop speakers and headphones sound bigger on Windows

Why PC audio so often sounds small and flat, the free fixes already in Windows, and how to add width and weight without distortion.

By Hasnain Butt Akhtar · XLUNA™ Studio · 19 October 2026

Most of what makes PC audio sound small has nothing to do with how expensive your speakers or headphones were. It comes from a few settings, one Bluetooth habit and the physics of tiny drivers. Fix those in order and the same hardware sounds noticeably fuller.

Why it sounds small in the first place

Laptop speakers are a few centimetres across and sit inside a thin case, so they physically cannot move enough air to reproduce deep bass. The sound also comes from almost the same point, which makes stereo feel narrow. Headphones have the opposite problem: the bass is there, but music mixed for speakers in a room can feel like it is happening inside your head rather than around you.

First, rule out the Bluetooth trap

If your wireless headphones suddenly sound thin, muffled or like a phone call, check whether something is using their microphone. On most Bluetooth headsets, when an app opens the microphone, Windows switches the headset into its hands free mode, which carries both directions of sound at much lower quality and often in mono. A meeting app left open in the background is enough to do it.

The fix is to close the app holding the microphone, or to choose your PC's own microphone in that app, so the headset can return to its full quality stereo mode.

Free fixes already in Windows

  1. Check the source. A low quality stream sounds small whatever you do to it. In music services, set streaming quality to high or very high, and play local files at their original quality.
  2. Loudness equalisation. On many PCs the classic Sound control panel offers it under the speaker's properties, on an Enhancements or Advanced tab, depending on your audio driver. It evens out quiet and loud passages, which helps films with quiet dialogue on small speakers.
  3. Spatial sound for headphones. Windows includes Windows Sonic for Headphones. Find it in Settings, System, Sound, under your headphones' properties, as Spatial sound. It places stereo and surround content around you instead of between your ears.
  4. Leave headroom. Running Windows at full volume and then boosting inside an app is the quickest route to distortion on laptop speakers. Keep some room at the top.

Adding bass and width without distortion

The instinct is to push the bass slider up, but on small speakers that mostly produces rattle and crackle, because the drivers cannot reproduce those frequencies at volume. Effective enhancement works around that:

  • Lift the upper bass, not the deepest bass. A gentle boost where small speakers can actually respond gives a feeling of weight without asking for what they cannot deliver.
  • Widen the stereo image. Spreading sound beyond the speakers or around the head is what most people mean by bigger sound, and it costs nothing in loudness.
  • Use a limiter. A limiter catches the loudest moments, explosions or a sudden chorus, before they clip, so you can enjoy more enhancement at a comfortable volume.
  • Compare at the same loudness. Louder always sounds better at first, which makes it easy to fool yourself. A fair before and after keeps both at the same level.
